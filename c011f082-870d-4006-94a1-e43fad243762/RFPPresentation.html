Appendix

How we determine jurisdiction, and apply its rules

Resolved per message, not per campaign. Comply re-derives the ruleset every time we contact a customer.

Step 1
Locate the customer
Residential address on the placement file is the primary determinant, validated and standardized at ingestion. Phone number and correspondence address are checked against it, and a mismatch flags the account for review rather than guessing.
Step 2
Resolve the ruleset
Rules cascade global, then country, then state or province. The most specific rule wins, and your own policy layers on top of the statutory floor.
Step 3
Evaluate before sending
Every planned message goes to Comply’s firewall and is evaluated against the resolved ruleset. Pass and it delivers. Violate and it is blocked, with the reason recorded in plain language.
Step 4
Re-derive on change
A customer who moves state is re-evaluated on the next contact, not the next campaign. Every decision is logged in machine-readable form and exportable for your auditors.
What Comply checks, in nine categories
Timing and quiet hours Contact frequency Blackout dates Consent and opt-out Language and tone Vulnerability signals Sentiment Complaint indicators Dispute signals

Rules are written in plain English by our compliance specialists, so a regulatory change or a Verizon policy change is authored and live without an engineering release.

Your policy can be stricter than the law

Comply holds a separate rule layer for each client. One of our BNPL clients already runs one in the US: SMS is restricted as a matter of their internal policy even though it is legal in the jurisdiction, and the engine respects that everywhere it applies.

A separate Comply instance, database and ruleset runs in each region: Sydney, Virginia, Toronto and London.
