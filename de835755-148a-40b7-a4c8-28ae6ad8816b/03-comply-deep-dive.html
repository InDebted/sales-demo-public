Four checkpoints in a customer’s story.

Comply shows up four times in every customer interaction. Before you reach out. When they answer back. How they’re letting you talk to them. What was said last week.

One decision engine, four moments — every decision lands in the same audit trail with the same legal citations. That’s where the value sits for IAG: demonstrable evidence of vulnerable-customer identification, closing the loop between an obligation and an action on a specific customer, and shifting from reactive complaints to proactive intervention. Front-line staff catch what they can. Comply catches what they can’t.

How Safeguard Classifies Messages ▼

Send any inbound customer message to the Safeguard endpoint. Comply classifies the text and returns a structured decision with signal type, confidence score, explanation, and recommended action.

⚠
ALLOW_WITH_CONDITIONS ▼

Not every evaluation is a binary ALLOW or BLOCK. Some messages are allowed but with conditions noted — for example, required disclosures that must be present, or frequency limits that are approaching their threshold. 

→ ALLOW_WITH_CONDITIONS
  Rule: au-hardship-disclosure (v2)
  Citation: General Insurance Code of Practice Pt.9
  Condition: "Message references payment arrangement but does
  not include required hardship disclosure statement. Message
  is allowed but condition logged for review."

  conditions: [
    {
      "type": "missing_disclosure",
      "description": "Hardship notice not present",
      "severity": "medium",
      "suggested_action": "append_disclosure"
    }
  ]

Conditions are delivered via webhook alongside the decision, giving your team visibility into near-misses and areas for template improvement.

Jurisdiction Hierarchy ▼

Rules cascade through a three-level hierarchy. More specific rules override general ones.

Level 1
Global
Universal compliance rules — apply everywhere
▼
Level 2
Country → State
AU rules apply to all states. NSW-specific rules override AU defaults.
▼
Level 3
Tenant-Specific
Client-specific rules can override defaults. E.g., an insurer may set tighter pre-action timing windows than the regulatory floor.
When a customer in NSW receives a message, Comply evaluates: Global rules → AU rules → AU:NSW rules → IAG tenant rules. Most specific match wins.

Regulatory Alignment & Results

ASIC RG 271

Hardship identification

Licensees must identify customers experiencing financial difficulty and respond with appropriate assistance. Comply’s Safeguard detects hardship signals automatically at the point of contact — demonstrable evidence that the identification obligation was met for every customer, on every channel.

NCCP Act

Outbound contact conduct

Governs how customers in financial difficulty may be contacted. Comply enforces contact timing windows, frequency limits, and required-disclosure language pre-send — preventing the breach instead of investigating it after the fact.

General Insurance Code of Practice

Vulnerability & hardship (Pt.9 & Pt.10)

Sets the bar for vulnerable-customer handling and financial hardship across general insurance. Comply identifies family violence, elder abuse, claims-related crisis, and hardship signals — automatically activating the protocols GICOP requires.
