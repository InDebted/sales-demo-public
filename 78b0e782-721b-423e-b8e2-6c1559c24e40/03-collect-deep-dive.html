What this illustrates

Collect adapted to the signals it was seeing. When SMS came back undelivered, it was dropped from the channel mix. When opens started rising on email, the send slot was pulled earlier and earlier into the customer's day until they engaged. When the first payment failed, the engine waited rather than nudge again immediately — and recovered the account three days later. The point isn't that email beats SMS or phone. It's that the system chose the right next action based on what the customer was actually doing.

If a bank looked only at a flat activity report

Three reasonable questions would surface: why was there no call? why were emails still being sent after a failed payment? why were there gaps between actions? Standard reporting shows what was sent and when. It doesn't show what the system was reading — open patterns, send-time fit, the failed-charge cooldown. Visible activity can look passive while live engagement is happening underneath.

Traditional agencies apply a deterministic workflow — call, chase, repeat. Collect makes decisions from events and engagement signals. We don't judge success by whether the same action ran on every account; we judge it by whether the system chose the right next action. Some accounts should stay longer in Collect for exactly this reason — active engagement may already be in motion in ways the bank's standard reports won't surface.

The plumbing under the engine

Everything you've just seen — the decisions, the AI generation, the multi-channel orchestration — runs on a single architectural choice. There is no customer table. Everything is an event.

Event-driven · 313 handlers · 170+ services

Where the operating model can be strengthened

Three areas worth working through together

Three friction points we typically see in traditional collections operations — each one solvable, and each one a lift in customer experience and recovery once addressed.

1
File referral
Stage 02 · Data exchange
✕ What's happening today
A column header in the referral file changes between releases — say "OutstandingBal" becomes "OutstandingBalance". Ingestion fails and emits an error file, which then requires manual review and re-processing by both teams before those accounts can enter the lifecycle.
Recovery: manual re-load, manual reconciliation. Result: delays in collection on the affected batch.
✓ Collect's optimum handling
Adhere to the Collect file spec as an API contract — schema agreed and version-locked between client and InDebted. Monitor for error files and respond accordingly — clear ownership for each side, structured remediation when an error does occur.
Outcome: schema drift caught at the boundary. Errors are handled, not absorbed.
2
Payment plans & settlements
Stage 03 / 07 · Collections + payments
✕ What's happening today
A customer asks for a plan or settlement. The request goes through an approval loop, then we have to re-engage the customer once it comes back. By the time we reach them again, the moment may have passed.
Result: lost momentum. Plans agreed in principle that never get set up because the customer has disengaged.
✓ Collect's optimum handling
Pre-approved plan durations, monthly settlement budget, or per-account settlement authority — defined once during scoping, applied automatically. Plans and discounts are immediately available when the customer is engaged and ready to commit. No re-engagement loop required.
Outcome: the conversion happens at peak intent, not days later.
3
Balance updates
Stage 02 / 03 · Data exchange + collections
✕ What's happening today
When account refreshes come through, the file doesn't include the underlying transactions — only the new balance. This forces manual reconciliation on the InDebted side every time a balance changes.
Knock-on effects: customers who've already agreed plans under the original balance can't simply have the extra owed taken without re-engagement. And explaining to a customer why their balance changed requires the underlying transactions we don't have.
✓ Collect's optimum handling
Every balance change driven by a transaction — interest, fees, direct payments, adjustments. Same principle a bank's own ledger runs on. Collect ingests the transaction, the balance recomputes, and the change is fully traceable.
Result: InDebted can always explain to the customer why their balance has changed — and active plans adjust on the actual movement, not a silent overwrite.

Where humans still do the work

To be clear: this isn't a fully unmanned operation. When the engine signals it can't do better digitally, work gets routed to our agents. Three modes:

Mode 01

Cold-trail outbound

Accounts where digital channels have stopped engaging — emails not opening, SMS undelivered. Agents call within compliant windows.

Mode 02

Inbound complex intent

Customer calls, raises a dispute, asks for hardship terms outside pre-approved thresholds. Live agent picks up with full account context.

Mode 03

Triage on signals

Vulnerable-customer signal, deceased indicator, fraud flag — engine pauses outbound and routes to a trained specialist queue.
