01 Account upload / data exchange
01.1
File format
A single unified file per portfolio, in the format agreed during scoping. Files land on our SFTP daily; structured errors flow back the same way.
A consistent format per drop is what enables straight-through processing — every minute spent fixing files is a minute not spent on accounts.
Agree the file format and field mapping during scoping. Drop files on our SFTP in the agreed layout.
01.2
Column naming & data quality
Field-level validation matters: 'account number' vs 'card number', amounts in the right fields. Once Collect ingests, those fields drive treatment logic.
Mislabelled fields block automation. Auto-upload only stays on when files are clean; corrections after load distort treatment and reporting.
Validate field names and data quality before files leave your systems. This is what keeps auto-upload running.
01.3
Data integrity at handoff
Account numbers and balances loaded into Collect can't be corrected in place once ingested. Workarounds via manual transaction uploads distort account history.
Manual cleanup per account. Misleading records affect treatment logic and reporting.
Confirm data integrity before upload — pre-upload sign-off catches the rare bad batch.
01.4
Required data fields
Treatment quality depends on having last payment date, DOB (for credit-card customers), and a linked-liabilities indicator on every account.
Treatment sequencing weakens without last payment date; tracing is harder without DOB. No visibility on linked liabilities means joint-settlement requirements can't be anticipated.
Include these fields in the upload schema. The linked-liabilities flag is the most operationally important.
01.5
Balance changes carry transactions
When balances change (bank fees, interest, adjustments), Collect needs the supporting transaction record so account history stays accurate.
Updated-balance-only feeds create mismatches between Collect and your core banking — and force re-uploads that lose the change context.
Pair every balance change with a transaction record. No silent overwrites.
02 Reporting
02.1
Payment report
Daily payment reports delivered T+1, by 10am local time, in the agreed format (encryption, decimal handling, file structure).
Standard reconciliation cadence — finance and ops teams plan around it.
Agree report format and delivery window during scoping. We accommodate compliance requirements (e.g. encryption-at-rest).
02.2
Real-time payment view
Stripe dashboard access gives nominated teams real-time visibility on inbound payments. Beyond that, T+1 is the fastest standardised written report.
The visibility gap between customer payment and finance seeing it drives reconciliation friction if it isn't planned for.
Identify which teams need real-time visibility (typically ops and finance) — we'll wire the access during onboarding.
02.3
Payment file integrity
Payment files are deduplicated at source and validated on ingest. Same payment seen twice would distort commission and reconciliation.
Duplicates cascade through commission calculations and reconciliation; if they slip in, auto-upload pauses until they're cleared.
Deduplicate at source. This keeps auto-upload of payment files running.
02.4
Payout cadence (Stripe)
Stripe settlement timing is set by the local Stripe market. Most regions are T+2; some (e.g. UAE) are T+5 business days. Payout reports are paced to actual settlement.
The cycle between customer payment and money landing in your account affects cycle-end and month-end reconciliation.
Plan internal cycle-end and month-end cut-offs around the local settlement window. We'll publish the actual cadence for your market during scoping.
02.5
Activity report
Daily activity reports cover calls, SMS, emails — informing operational reviews and audit. Target lag from event to report is 1–2 days.
Reflects what's actually happening across all channels — without it, ops reviews are guesswork.
Align on which teams own activity-report consumption (ops + risk).
03 Payments
03.1
Settlement timing
Stripe is our payment processor; settlement timing is set by the local Stripe market — typically T+2, sometimes longer (T+5 in some markets). Outside our control, but predictable.
You can't reconcile against core banking until the money physically lands. Impacts cycle-end and month-end cut-offs if not planned for.
Confirm internal cut-off behaviour during scoping. Some clients update customer balances at the InDebted-recorded payment event; others wait for landed funds. Both work — pick the one your finance team prefers.

04
Late-stage classification
Late-stage delinquency (typically 5+, 6+) functionally rolls regardless of treatment. We work these as recoveries, not active collections — payment plans and settlement offers are the right tools.
Treating late-stage as active collections forces tools (no settlements) that don't fit the customer's actual position.
Reclassify late-stage as recoveries. Lock interest accrual. Allow Collect to work them with payment plans + settlements (typically ≥180 DPD).
05
Settlement authority
Settlement is a core lever. Two patterns we've seen work: a delegated authority grid (% discount × DPD × balance band), or a monthly settlement budget (aggregate envelope per allocation).
Without delegated authority, every settlement waits for approval — deals are lost while waiting.
With it, InDebted deploys capital where it works hardest; offers that would lift overall recovery actually go out.
Agree a settlement framework before pilot starts.
We strongly recommend the monthly Settlement Budget pattern (e.g. AED 250k aggregate / month). InDebted decides per-account use within the envelope; any overage is netted from the invoice. Maximises portfolio performance with a clear ceiling.
06
Shared KPIs
We measure on a shared scorecard agreed before pilot starts — placements, contacts, payments, settlements, spin-down.
Without shared definitions, both sides measure different things. Spin-down reports across agencies give a like-for-like benchmark.
Agree the KPI suite before pilot launch. Provide spin-down reports from incumbent agencies if available — we'll match them.
07
Account retention
Account-recall windows shape how the engine performs. Many traditional models recall and rotate after 30 days — too short for digital-first treatment.
30 days prevents Collect from optimising contact time and channel per consumer — the engine has barely learned what works before the account is pulled.
Engaged customers — opening messages, visiting the portal, on a payment plan — get recalled before they can complete payment. Especially impactful where consumers are paid monthly: a salary date outside the 30-day window means the customer can't action.
The next agency inherits no context, the customer is less likely to re-engage, PTPs in flight are lost.
Allow Collect to retain accounts across multiple months by default — and longer where the consumer is showing meaningful engagement. The engine optimises per consumer; engagement converts to payment when given the time to play out.
