Section 03 · Collect

Built for the volume profile of tolling.
Not retrofitted onto it.

Most unpaid-toll balances sit in the sub-thousand-dollar range where digital collections clearly out-performs the traditional letter/call/agency motion. But unlike a telco, you can't disconnect a car from the road — so a long tail of larger balances builds up that needs a different handling shape. The platform has to do both, and not turn either into a headline.

The volume profile

Two cohorts, not one. Both need the platform.

The framing below is a qualitative read of the tolling profile, not a Transurban-supplied figure. Real numbers would need to come from your data.

Cohort A · the bulk
Sub-thousand-dollar balances
Small enough that an agent-handled letter/call cycle costs more than the debt itself. Large enough that a self-serve digital prompt with a one-tap payment path reliably converts. This is the volume of the problem, and it's exactly the cohort digital collections was built for.
Cohort B · the long tail
Balances above the sweet spot
Toll operators can't disconnect a car the way a telco can disconnect a phone. Account-holders with multiple vehicles, fleet usage, or long unaddressed periods can accumulate balances that need a different handling shape — softer-touch, longer plans, hardship-aware — not the same cadence stretched louder.
The implication: the platform has to do both — the digital lift on the bulk, and a softer-touch escalation on the long tail — and not turn the long-tail conversation into the headline-generating event. A traditional letter/call/agency motion handles the long tail poorly and the bulk uneconomically; that's the gap the platform is built for.
What Collect actually does

The four things the engine does on every customer, every day.

Collect is a decisioning engine wrapped in a multi-channel comms platform. These are the four moves it makes — and the four moves the traditional model can't.

01

Multi-channel orchestration

Reach customers across SMS, email, voice, web, and customer portal — with the platform owning the “next best channel” decision per customer, not the agent.

  • One outbound rail across every channel — not one tool per channel
  • Engagement signals (deliverability, open, response) feed the next decision
  • Channel-of-last-success bias — talk to people where they actually talk back
  • Runs under the InDebted brand — the independent-escalation step after Linkt's own contact
02

Behaviour-led timing and content

Models trained on a decade of recovery interactions decide when to send, how often, and which content variant to pick — instead of a static templated cadence.

  • Time-of-day + day-of-week tuned per customer's engagement history
  • Content variants tested continuously rather than re-templated annually
  • Hardship signals route off the standard cadence into Safeguard (see next section)
  • The same customer doesn't get the same message twice if the first didn't land
03

Self-serve resolution layer

The cheapest customer interaction is the one that didn't need staff. Customer portal is built so “pay now,” “set up a plan,” “dispute the trip,” and “tell us you're struggling” are all one-tap journeys — including in the SMS link, not just on the portal.

  • One-tap pay (Apple Pay, Google Pay, card, BPAY)
  • Self-serve payment plans with affordability bands
  • Dispute / vehicle-not-mine flows that route to the right back-office team
  • Vulnerability self-declaration with sensitive-handling routing
04

Closed-loop learning

Every interaction feeds back into the model. What worked on similar customers last quarter is what the platform tries first this quarter — rather than running last year's letter template against this year's cohort.

  • Recovery vs cost-to-resolve tracked per cohort
  • Complaint and dispute signals fed back as a guardrail, not just a KPI
  • Continuous model updates rather than annual template review
  • Operator dashboards on the same data the model sees

Counterfactual What a traditional escalation path tends to do
Without an independent digital escalation step, the next move is typically a paper notice with an admin fee, then an agency referral, then state-revenue handover. End-to-end cost on a $24.30 debt commonly exceeds the debt itself.
The difference: Same customer. Same balance. The independent digital step is meant to resolve the easy ones before the expensive, brand-exposed path begins.
How Collect protects the Linkt brand

Independence is the point, not the side-effect.

Toll roads carry brand exposure that other verticals don't — fines become news stories, robo-calls become tweets. The two-brand pathway is how Collect is designed to handle that.

L→I

Two brands, one journey

Linkt's name stays on the in-brand reminders (Receive). The firmer escalation arrives from a recognisably separate sender (InDebted). That separation is itself a tested signal — and it keeps Linkt out of the part of the conversation most likely to generate a screenshot.

T

Tone tuned to context

Content variants are chosen for the customer and the stage — not a single template fired at every cohort. The aim is to avoid both the “debt collector” tone and the “passive ping” tone that misses the cohort entirely.

G

Complaint signal as a guardrail

Recovery rate isn't read in isolation. Complaint and dispute volume from the same cohort gets read alongside it, so a lift in recovery that comes with a spike in complaints is a problem the operating team sees, not a number the dashboard hides.

The headline metric isn't recovery rate alone — it's the gap between recovery rate and complaint rate. If recovery lifts and complaints lift with it, that's not a win worth claiming. The platform is built to make that trade-off measurable, not assumed.
