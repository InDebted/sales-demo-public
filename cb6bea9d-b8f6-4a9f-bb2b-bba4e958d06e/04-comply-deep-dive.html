Section 04 · Comply

The layer that makes “balanced, simpler, more transparent” demonstrable.
On every customer. Every day.

NSW Toll Reform doesn't ask whether you have a policy. It asks whether you can show, on a specific customer, on a specific day, in a specific channel, that the right rules were applied, the vulnerability signals were caught, and the audit trail is intact. Comply is the layer that makes that evidence a by-product of every interaction — not a quarterly forensic exercise.

What the reform actually asks

The NSW Toll Reform agenda, mapped to what Comply provides.

The Dec 2025 NSW Toll Reform announcement uses three words repeatedly: balanced, simpler, transparent. Each of those translates into a specific operational obligation. Each obligation maps to a Comply capability.

Reform asks → Platform provides

Three obligations the agenda is making concrete, and the Comply mechanism that turns each one into evidence rather than rhetoric.

Source · NSW Govt, Dec 2025
The ask · Balanced
Recovery action proportionate to circumstance — hardship and vulnerability identified at the point of contact, not after escalation.
→
The mechanism · Safeguard
Real-time vulnerability scoring on inbound messages, outbound copy, and customer behaviour. Hardship cohorts auto-routed off the standard cadence into a softer track, with a documented decision.
The ask · Simpler
Customers should be able to understand what they owe, why, and what their options are — without parsing four notices and a fee schedule.
→
The mechanism · Rules + Consent
Rule library enforces plain-language, one-message-one-action standards. Consent layer tracks what the customer has agreed to (channel, frequency, contact times) and blocks anything that contradicts it before send.
The ask · Transparent
A regulator, ombudsman, or journalist asks for the full history on a single customer. You can produce it the same day, not after a forensic project.
→
The mechanism · Audit
One-customer-one-timeline view of every message sent, rule applied, signal raised, decision taken, and human override — exportable to PDF or API. The evidence is a by-product, not a project.
The five pillars

What Comply actually is, behind the brochure.

Five components, each doing a discrete job. None depends on you adopting Collect. All five can wrap your existing Linkt outbound on day one.

01

Firewall

Pre-send rule-check on every outbound message — SMS, email, voice script, letter. Blocks or auto-amends anything that fails a rule before it reaches the customer.

When it fires: milliseconds before send
02

Safeguard

Real-time vulnerability identification from inbound text, voice, and behaviour. Routes the customer off the standard cadence into a sensitive-handling track with a documented reason.

When it fires: on every interaction
03

Rules

160+ jurisdiction-mapped rules, maintained by a dedicated legal team. Covers NSW Toll Reform, National Credit Code, Privacy Act, ACMA Spam Act, AFCA, and the state revenue regimes.

When it fires: continuously, version-controlled
04

Audit

One-customer-one-timeline view of every event. Filterable, exportable, queryable. The evidence layer for regulator queries, ombudsman complaints, and journalist questions.

When it fires: always-on; query on demand
05

Consent

Tracks every consent given, withdrawn, or implied — channel, frequency, hours, language. Blocks any outbound that would violate a consent, before it's queued.

When it fires: pre-queue + on-update
Worked scenario

A vulnerable-customer journey, walked end-to-end.

Same shape as the Collect scenario, but for the customer who should never have got the standard cadence. What the five pillars do — together — to catch the signal, divert the cadence, and produce the evidence.

David, two trips, $19.80 — but the conversation has changed.

Brisbane account holder. Two unpaid trips. Replies to the first SMS with: “I lost my job last week, I can't pay this right now, please don't keep messaging me.” A signal arrives. The platform has to act on it — and evidence it.

Safeguard · NLU scoring
Inbound SMS scored: hardship signal 0.94, frequency-fatigue signal 0.71, no-contact-request signal 0.88. Three signals, all over threshold, on one message.
What it caught: “I lost my job” is hardship. “Please don't keep messaging me” is a contact-frequency preference and a partial consent withdrawal. The platform doesn't need the customer to say “I'm in hardship” verbatim.

cadence change
2
Consent · auto-update
Outbound contact frequency reduced from “standard” to “sensitive-handling”. SMS cadence dropped from 14-day to 30-day. Voice + push channels disabled until reviewed. Decision logged.
What it produced: The customer's preference is now binding on the platform. Anything trying to send to David outside the sensitive-handling track gets blocked at queue-time — even if a different team starts a different campaign.
00:01
cohort move
3
Safeguard · cohort routing
David moved to hardship cohort. Standard Collect cadence cancelled. Hardship cadence offers an interest-frozen plan, a longer payment window, and a clear human-contact option. No admin fee accrues.
What it avoided: The next standard touch would have been a $14 admin fee notice. The cohort move prevented it — and the prevention itself is logged as an event, so the question “why wasn't this customer charged the fee?” has an answer on the timeline.
02:00
response
4
Firewall · pre-send check
A reply SMS is drafted: “Thanks for letting us know, David — we've paused the messages and put your account on hold for 30 days. When you're ready, here's a no-fee payment plan.” Firewall passes it — plain-language ✓, no fee mention without amount ✓, no implied urgency ✓.
What it checked: 17 rules including NSW Toll Reform plain-language standard, National Credit Code s.86 hardship language, Privacy Act notice handling, ACMA Spam Act unsubscribe, and the AFCA hardship-response template.

If a regulator, ombudsman, or journalist asks tomorrow: “How did Linkt handle David?”, the answer is one click. Every signal, every decision, every message, every rule applied, every override, in one timeline. Export to PDF or pipe to API.

Adoption shape

Comply works two ways. Pick the one that fits.

You don't have to adopt Collect to adopt Comply. Most engagements with regulated incumbents start with Comply standalone — wrapping the existing outbound — and add the Collect engine after the evidence is in.

Lower-risk shape

Comply standalone

Comply wraps Linkt's existing recovery outbound. We don't touch your Collect motion, your back-office, or your customer-facing brand. We provide the Firewall, Safeguard, Rules, Audit, and Consent layers as an API your existing platform calls into.

  • Read-only Comply Audit on historical notices — week one
  • Firewall + Safeguard wired into a single outbound stream — month one
  • Full audit timeline for any customer — month two
  • No customer-facing change · no Linkt re-brand · low blast radius
Bigger-impact shape

Comply + Collect together

The combined platform — Collect engine wrapped by Comply layer — is where the brand and recovery economics step-change. The path there is typically incremental, with each step justified by the evidence the previous one produced.

  • Collect runs a defined cohort (e.g. one region, one stage)
  • Comply provides the audit + rule wrap on the same cohort
  • Compare recovery, complaint, sentiment vs control
  • Expand cohort by cohort as evidence justifies
